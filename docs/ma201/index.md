# MA201: Calculus & Optimization

Welcome to **MA201: Calculus & Optimization**!

In computational biology and machine learning, calculus is not an exercise in algebraic manipulation—it is the engine behind **finding optimal model parameters**.

Whether you are fitting a Michaelis-Menten enzyme kinetics curve, performing Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) for phylogenetic tree branch lengths, or training deep neural networks for protein folding, calculus tells us **which direction to adjust parameters to maximize likelihood or minimize error**.

---

## Course Modules

1. [**Single-Variable Differentiation & Extremum Points**](single-variable-calculus.html)
   * *Rationale*: Instant rates of change, core differentiation rules (Power, Product, Quotient, Chain rules), and finding critical points ($f'(x) = 0$).
2. [**Multivariable Calculus & The Gradient Vector**](multivariable-calculus.html)
   * *Rationale*: Partial derivatives ($\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}$) and why the gradient vector $\nabla f$ points in the direction of steepest increase.
3. [**Multivariable Optimization & The Hessian Matrix**](optimization-and-hessian.html)
   * *Rationale*: Finding critical points in higher dimensions ($\nabla f = \mathbf{0}$), testing the Hessian matrix for Positive Semi-Definiteness (PSD) to classify local minima vs. maxima, and understanding Gradient Descent.
